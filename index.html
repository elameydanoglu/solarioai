The problem

Sunlight is free. It just isn't constant.

Solar energy holds great promise as a renewable resource, yet the sunlight it depends on shifts with the weather and the seasons. Households, schools and small businesses often struggle to predict how much energy their panels will produce on any given day.

Could machine learning, trained on historical weather data, help people anticipate these fluctuations in advance?

We chose Istanbul as our starting point. Picture a school that is considering solar panels: by entering recent weather and the panels it has in mind, it gets a realistic picture of how much solar electricity it might have the next day.

What the model pays attention to

Feature importance from the Random Forest running on this page. The time of year sets the overall level of sunlight, and today's radiation adjusts it for the current weather pattern. Cloud cover and the previous days fine-tune the result.

How it works

From ten years of weather to a forecast you can use.

  1. Data

    Historical weather and solar radiation for Istanbul, 2015–2025, from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API.

  2. Preprocessing

    Daily records, radiation converted from MJ/m² to kWh/m², plus features for seasonal cycles and previous days' radiation.

  3. Model

    A Random Forest Regressor combines many decision trees to learn patterns from the historical data.

  4. Train & validate

    Trained on 2015–2023, compared on 2024. The chosen model was retrained with 2024 included and evaluated on 2025.

  5. Application

    This website runs the trained model in your browser: enter weather and panel details to get tomorrow's radiation and energy estimate.

Where it fails

What SolarioAI can't do yet.

Sudden weather

The model can't reliably foresee abrupt changes such as unexpected cloud cover or storms.

Observed inputs

The predictor needs a day's observed weather and radiation. It doesn't read weather forecasts, so it predicts one day ahead.

Simplified energy

The electricity estimate ignores shading, panel orientation, heat-related efficiency losses and other real-world factors.

2025 results were looked at during earlier development, so the final evaluation isn't fully independent of prior decisions. That's why this site also tests the model on 2026 data, which played no part in any decision.

What we'd do next

Where SolarioAI goes from here.

Live weather forecasts

Pull weather data automatically so predictions don't depend on manual entry.

Beyond Istanbul

Train and evaluate on other cities to see whether the approach holds in different climates.

Better energy estimates

Account for panel orientation, shading, temperature effects and system losses.

Keep evaluating
